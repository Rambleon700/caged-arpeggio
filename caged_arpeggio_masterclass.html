Sweep Picking Mechanics & Rest Strokes

Sweep picking relies on a single continuous motion across the strings rather than individual picking strokes. Think of your plectrum as brushing across a set of harp strings.

1. Pick Slanting (45° Angle): Incline the pick forward in the direction of stroke travel to push strings down with minimal friction.
2. Right-Hand Palm Muting: Follow immediately behind the picking motion with the fleshy edge of your picking hand palm to deaden low strings.
3. Synchronization Rule: Only one note must ring at a time. Lift fretting fingers immediately after sounding to avoid chord bleeding.

String Skipping & Wide Interval Traversal

String skipping bypasses adjacent strings to create wide octave leaps, pedal point patterns, and modern pianistic arpeggio shapes.

Left-Hand Muting Shield: Lightly rest index finger flesh across unplayed skipped strings to neutralize unwanted buzz.
Pick Trajectory: Use small outside-picking motions when jumping over middle strings.

Two-Handed Tapping & Legato Arpeggios

Combining right-hand tapping with left-hand hammer-ons and pull-offs creates fluid 3-octave arpeggio waterfalls at lightning speeds.

Right-Hand Flick: Tap squarely behind the fret wire with middle or index finger, then flick sideways slightly down toward the floor to pluck the next note.
Hammer-on from Nowhere: Drive left-hand fingers onto higher strings with crisp velocity without picking.

Connecting the Fretboard: Horizontal, Vertical & Diagonal

Unlock complete fretboard freedom by transitioning between CAGED positions seamlessly.

1. Horizontal Linear Navigation

Play arpeggio tones along 1 or 2 single strings up the neck. Excellent for vocal-like slides and expressive phrasing.

2. Vertical Box Navigation

Stay strictly within a single CAGED position across all 6 strings. Maximum speed, minimal hand shift across frets.

3. Diagonal 3-Octave Navigation

The ultimate technique: slide diagonally across CAGED positions as you ascend strings to cover the entire 24 frets in one run.
